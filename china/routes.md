# PRC export routes for personal information

**Content verification date: 2026-09-13.** All article numbers, thresholds, dates
and deadlines below were checked against the sources listed in §7 on that date.
Recheck before relying on any of it.

Label key: `[Statutory]` the instrument says this · `[Interpretive]` a defensible
reading or a regulator's published guidance, not statutory text · `[Unsettled]`
genuinely open. Two further markers appear: `[Dynamic — retrieve current version]`
for material that changes without any instrument being amended, and
`[To be verified]` for a statement not confirmed against a source retrieved on the
verification date, with a note on what would confirm it.

## 1. The instruments in force

**Three laws**

- **《中华人民共和国网络安全法》** (Cybersecurity Law, "CSL") — adopted
  7 November 2016, amended by NPC Standing Committee decision of 28 October 2025,
  amendment in force 1 January 2026.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 39: operators of critical information infrastructure ("CIIOs")
  must store personal information and important data collected and generated in
  their operations within China; where it must be provided abroad for business
  need, a security assessment under measures made by the CAC with State Council
  departments applies.
  `[Statutory]` The amending decision states that the articles were renumbered and
  the law republished ("对条文顺序作相应调整，重新公布"). **This provision is
  Article 39 in the text in force. It is Article 37 in the 2016 text and is still
  widely cited that way.** Art. 37 in the current text is the procurement security
  review provision.

- **《中华人民共和国数据安全法》** (Data Security Law, "DSL") — adopted
  10 June 2021, in force 1 September 2021.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 21: the State establishes a classified and graded data
  protection system; regions and departments determine important data catalogues.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 31: export security management of important data collected and
  generated by CIIOs is governed by the CSL; for other data processors, measures
  are made by the CAC together with relevant State Council departments.

- **《中华人民共和国个人信息保护法》** (Personal Information Protection Law,
  "PIPL") — adopted 20 August 2021, in force 1 November 2021.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 38: a processor that needs to provide personal information
  abroad must meet one of — (i) a CAC-organised security assessment under Art. 40;
  (ii) personal information protection certification by a professional body per
  CAC rules; (iii) a contract with the overseas recipient on the CAC standard
  contract; (iv) other conditions in laws, administrative regulations or CAC rules.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 39: separate consent, plus notice of the recipient's name,
  contact details, processing purpose, processing method, categories of personal
  information, and how the individual exercises PIPL rights against the recipient.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 40: CIIOs, and processors handling personal information
  reaching a volume specified by the CAC, must store personal information collected
  and generated in China within China.
  `[Unsettled]` The volume that triggers Art. 40 localisation for non-CIIOs. No
  instrument retrieved specifies it. Confirmation would be a CAC rule or
  announcement setting that number; none was found in the sources at §7.

**One administrative regulation**

- **《网络数据安全管理条例》** (Regulations on Network Data Security Management) —
  State Council Order No. 790, in force 1 January 2025.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 35 lists the conditions on which network data processors may
  provide personal information abroad: security assessment; certification by a
  professional body per CAC rules; a standard contract per CAC rules; necessity for
  concluding or performing a contract to which the individual is a party;
  cross-border human resources management under lawfully adopted labour rules and a
  lawfully concluded collective contract; necessity to perform statutory duties or
  obligations; emergency protection of natural persons' life, health and property
  safety; and other conditions set by law, administrative regulation or the CAC.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 37: important data collected and generated in operations in
  China that genuinely needs to be provided abroad must go through the
  CAC-organised security assessment.

**The CAC-level rules**

- **《促进和规范数据跨境流动规定》** (Provisions on Promoting and Regulating
  Cross-Border Data Flows) — CAC Order No. 16, published and in force
  22 March 2024. This is the operative routing instrument and the subject of most
  of this file.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 14: in force from the date of publication.
  `[Statutory]` Art. 13: where the 数据出境安全评估办法 (Order No. 11, published
  7 July 2022) and the 个人信息出境标准合同办法 (Order No. 13, published
  22 February 2023) are inconsistent with Order No. 16, Order No. 16 applies.

- **《数据出境安全评估办法》** (Measures for Security Assessment of Data Export) —
  CAC Order No. 11, in force 1 September 2022. Procedure for the assessment route.
  Superseded on points of inconsistency by Order No. 16.

- **《个人信息出境标准合同办法》** (Measures for Standard Contracts for the Export
  of Personal Information) — CAC Order No. 13, in force 1 June 2023. Procedure and
  filing for the standard contract route. Superseded on points of inconsistency by
  Order No. 16.

- **《个人信息出境认证办法》** (Measures for Personal Information Export
  Certification) — joint CAC and 国家市场监督管理总局 (State Administration for
  Market Regulation, "SAMR") Order No. 20, in force 1 January 2026 (Art. 19).
  This completed the certification route, which until then operated on standards
  and pilot practice rather than a dedicated rule.

## 2. Order of analysis

Run these in order. Each step can dispose of the question, and running them out
of order produces the wrong route.

**Step 1 — Classify the data.** Is there personal information? Is there sensitive
personal information? Is there important data? Is the exporter a CIIO?

`[Statutory]` Order No. 16, Art. 2: a data processor must identify and declare
important data in accordance with the relevant rules; where it has **not** been
notified or publicly announced as important data by the relevant department or
region, the processor need not declare it as important data for the purposes of a
security assessment.

`[Interpretive]` This is a notification-based safe harbour for the *declaration*
step, not a statement that unnotified data is not important data. The CAC policy
Q&A of April 2025 restates it in the same terms. The DSL Art. 21 catalogue system
continues to operate, and sectoral catalogues may bring data in later.

`[Unsettled]` Whether a processor that has not been notified, but whose data
plainly meets an announced sectoral description, can rely on Art. 2. The
instruments do not address it.

**Step 2 — Check the exemptions.** Order No. 16, Arts. 3–5. See §3. If an
exemption applies, no route is needed, but §5 obligations still apply.

**Step 3 — Check free trade zone negative lists.** Order No. 16, Art. 6. See §3.
This is checked after the general exemptions because it is location-specific and
narrower in who can use it.

**Step 4 — Route by threshold.** Order No. 16, Arts. 7–8. See §4.

`[Interpretive]` Steps 2 and 3 come before Step 4 because Arts. 3–6 are drafted as
exemptions from the requirement to use a route, not as alternative routes. A
transfer that qualifies under Art. 3, 4, 5 or 6 does not need to be assessed
against the Art. 7/8 thresholds at all.

## 3. The exemptions — 《促进和规范数据跨境流动规定》 (CAC Order No. 16, 22 March 2024), Arts. 3–5

Unless stated otherwise, each exemption below is an exemption from **all three**
routes: declaring a data export security assessment, concluding a personal
information export standard contract, and obtaining personal information
protection certification.

### Art. 3 — activity-based, no personal information and no important data

`[Statutory]` Data collected and generated in activities such as international
trade, cross-border transport, academic cooperation, transnational manufacturing
and marketing, provided abroad, where it contains no personal information and no
important data, is exempt from all three routes.

`[Interpretive]` The listed activities are introduced by 等 ("such as") and read as
illustrative rather than closed. The operative condition is the absence of personal
information and important data; the activity list describes the typical context.

### Art. 4 — re-export of personal information collected abroad

`[Statutory]` Where a data processor transmits personal information collected and
generated outside China into China for processing and then provides it abroad, and
no domestic personal information or important data was introduced in the
processing, all three routes are exempt.

`[Interpretive]` This is the provision that covers a China-based shared service or
processing centre handling foreign-origin data, provided the domestic pipeline
stays clean. Commingling with any China-collected personal information takes the
transfer out of Art. 4 entirely.

### Art. 5 — four condition-based exemptions for personal information

`[Statutory]` A processor providing personal information abroad is exempt from all
three routes where any one of the following applies:

1. It is genuinely necessary to provide the personal information abroad in order to
   conclude or perform a contract to which the individual is a party.
2. It is genuinely necessary to provide employees' personal information abroad in
   order to carry out cross-border human resources management under lawfully
   adopted labour rules and regulations and a lawfully concluded collective
   contract.
3. In an emergency, it is genuinely necessary to provide personal information
   abroad to protect the life, health or property safety of natural persons.
4. A processor **other than a CIIO** has cumulatively provided abroad, since
   1 January of the current year, personal information of **fewer than 100,000
   individuals**, excluding sensitive personal information.

`[Interpretive]` Art. 5(2) has two textual conditions that are easy to miss: the
transfer must rest on lawfully adopted internal labour rules **and** a lawfully
concluded collective contract (依法签订的集体合同). A group HR policy alone does not
obviously satisfy the second limb. The CAC policy Q&A of July 2026, addressing
recruitment scenarios, treats necessity as the controlling test — assessed by
relevance to the hiring process, the number of individuals affected and the scope
of fields — and states that where the overseas entity does not participate in the
hiring decision, necessity is not made out.

`[Unsettled]` Whether a collective contract is strictly required for Art. 5(2)
where no collective contract exists at the employer. The text is conjunctive;
practice is not uniform.

`[Unsettled]` How the Art. 5(4) cumulative count is measured — whether the
100,000 is counted per exporting entity or across a corporate group, whether the
same individual transferred repeatedly counts once or each time, and how transfers
to multiple recipients aggregate. The CAC policy Q&A of January 2026 addresses
what happens once a threshold is crossed, not how the count is constructed.

### Art. 6 — free trade zone negative lists

`[Statutory]` A 自由贸易试验区 (pilot free trade zone, "FTZ") may, within the
framework of the national classified and graded data protection system, draw up its
own list of data within the zone that must be brought within the scope of security
assessment, standard contract and certification management (the "negative list").
The list must be approved by the provincial cybersecurity and informatisation
commission and filed with the CAC and the national data administration. A data
processor **within** the FTZ providing data **not on** the negative list abroad may
be exempt from all three routes.

`[Dynamic — retrieve current version]` Negative lists are issued FTZ by FTZ, in
dated versions, and are revised. This reference does not state how many exist or
what is on any of them. Retrieve the current version from the issuing provincial or
municipal cyberspace administration for the specific FTZ. One example of the
published form is the Beijing FTZ negative list (2025 version), cited at §7.

`[Unsettled]` Whether a processor established outside an FTZ but transferring data
generated by its operations inside one can rely on that zone's list, and how a
group operating across several FTZs with different lists should proceed. The CAC
policy Q&A of April 2025 refers to coordination across zones but does not resolve
either question.

## 4. Threshold routing — Arts. 7–8

Where no exemption applies, the route is fixed by who the exporter is and what
volume of personal information it has cumulatively provided abroad since 1 January
of the current year.

### Art. 7 — security assessment required

`[Statutory]` A data processor must declare a data export security assessment to
the CAC, through the provincial cyberspace administration where it is located,
where either:

1. It is a **CIIO** providing personal information or important data abroad — no
   volume threshold applies; or
2. It is **not a CIIO** and it provides **important data** abroad, **or** has
   cumulatively provided abroad since 1 January of the current year the personal
   information of **1,000,000 individuals or more**, excluding sensitive personal
   information, **or** the sensitive personal information of **10,000 individuals
   or more**.

### Art. 8 — standard contract or certification

`[Statutory]` A processor other than a CIIO that has cumulatively provided abroad
since 1 January of the current year the personal information of **100,000 or more
but fewer than 1,000,000 individuals**, excluding sensitive personal information,
**or** the sensitive personal information of **fewer than 10,000 individuals**,
must either conclude a standard contract for personal information export with the
overseas recipient or obtain personal information protection certification.

`[Statutory]` 《个人信息出境认证办法》 (CAC/SAMR Order No. 20) Art. 5 restates
eligibility for the certification route in the same terms: the applicant must be a
non-CIIO **and** fall in the 100,000–1,000,000 non-sensitive or under-10,000
sensitive band.

`[Interpretive]` Standard contract and certification are alternatives within the
same band, not a sequence. The choice is operational — certification suits repeat
intra-group transfers under a single accountable structure; the standard contract
suits a defined bilateral transfer. Order No. 20 gives certification a dedicated
procedural rule for the first time, effective 1 January 2026.

`[Interpretive]` The eligibility thresholds in Order No. 13 Art. 4 (non-CIIO;
processing fewer than 1,000,000 individuals' personal information; cumulatively
fewer than 100,000 individuals since 1 January of the **preceding** year;
cumulatively fewer than 10,000 individuals' sensitive personal information since
1 January of the preceding year) are displaced by Order No. 16 Art. 8 on the points
of inconsistency, under Order No. 16 Art. 13. Order No. 16 counts from 1 January of
the **current** year and does not carry the "processing fewer than 1,000,000
individuals" limb.

`[Interpretive]` CAC policy Q&A, January 2026, on the interaction between the
standard contract, certification and the assessment: once cumulative exports from
1 January exceed 1,000,000 individuals (non-sensitive) or 10,000 individuals
(sensitive), the processor must declare a security assessment to the CAC through
its provincial cyberspace administration. This restates the Art. 7 threshold; the
threshold itself is statutory, the Q&A is guidance.

`[Unsettled]` What a processor must do mid-year when it crosses from the Art. 8
band into the Art. 7 band while a standard contract filing is already on foot —
in particular whether transfers must pause pending assessment. Not addressed in
the retrieved sources.

`[Unsettled]` Whether remote access from outside China to personal information
stored in China constitutes 数据出境 (data export) for these purposes. None of the
instruments retrieved defines 数据出境, and no retrieved CAC Q&A addresses remote
access. Confirmation would be a CAC rule or published Q&A item on the point.

## 5. Obligations that survive every exemption — Art. 10

`[Statutory]` Order No. 16, Art. 10: a data processor providing personal
information abroad must, in accordance with laws and administrative regulations,
perform the obligations of notification, obtaining the individual's **separate
consent**, and conducting a **personal information protection impact assessment**.

This sits outside Arts. 3–8. Qualifying for an exemption removes the route, not
these obligations.

`[Statutory]` The underlying obligations:
- Notice and separate consent — PIPL Art. 39. The notice must cover the overseas
  recipient's name and contact details, the processing purpose and method, the
  categories of personal information, and how the individual exercises PIPL rights
  against the recipient.
- Impact assessment — PIPL Art. 55(4) requires a personal information protection
  impact assessment before providing personal information abroad, with the
  processing recorded. PIPL Art. 56 sets its content: whether the purpose and
  method are lawful, justified and necessary; the impact on individuals' rights and
  the security risk; and whether the protective measures are lawful, effective and
  commensurate with the risk.

`[Statutory]` Order No. 16, Art. 11: a processor providing data abroad must comply
with laws and regulations, perform its data security protection obligations, and
take technical and other necessary measures to secure the export. Where a data
security incident occurs or may occur, it must take remedial measures and report
promptly to the provincial-or-above cyberspace administration and other competent
authorities.

`[Interpretive]` CAC policy Q&A, July 2026: separate consent must be specific and
unbundled — a single omnibus consent ("一揽子") is not acceptable — and may be
collected by signature, pop-up confirmation, email or SMS. For sensitive personal
information the notice must additionally explain the necessity of processing and
the impact on the individual. Where the processing rests on a PIPL Art. 13(1)(ii)–
(vii) basis rather than consent, separate consent is not required but the
notification obligation remains.

`[Interpretive]` Practical consequence: an Art. 5(4) "under 100,000" exporter still
owes notice, separate consent and a documented impact assessment. Treating the
exemption as a compliance exit is the most common error in this area.

## 6. Validity and re-filing

### Security assessment

`[Statutory]` Order No. 16, Art. 9: the result of a data export security assessment
is valid for **3 years** from the date it is issued. Where the processor needs to
continue the export after expiry and no circumstance requiring a fresh declaration
has arisen, it may apply to the CAC through its provincial cyberspace
administration to extend the validity period, **within the 60 working days before
expiry**.

`[Statutory]` Order No. 11, Art. 14 set the validity period at **2 years** and
required a fresh declaration 60 working days before expiry. Under Order No. 16
Art. 13, Order No. 16 prevails: the period in force is 3 years and the mechanism is
extension rather than re-declaration.

`[Statutory]` Order No. 11, Art. 14 also lists the circumstances requiring a fresh
declaration **within** the validity period:
1. A change in the purpose, method, scope or categories of the data provided
   abroad, or in the recipient's purpose or method of processing, affecting the
   security of the exported data; or an extension of the overseas retention period
   for personal information or important data.
2. A change in the data security laws, policies or network security environment of
   the recipient's country or region; force majeure; a change in actual control of
   the processor or the recipient; or a change to the legal documents between them,
   affecting the security of the exported data.
3. Any other circumstance affecting the security of the exported data.

`[Interpretive]` These triggers are not inconsistent with Order No. 16 and continue
to apply. Order No. 16 displaces the validity period and the end-of-term mechanism,
not the in-term re-declaration triggers.

`[Interpretive]` CAC policy Q&A, July 2026, on extension eligibility — the
conditions reported are: the purpose and scope of the export are unchanged; the
processor and the overseas recipient are unchanged; for personal information, the
projected volume over the next three years does not exceed the volume approved for
the previous three years by more than 20%; for important data, the projected scale
likewise does not exceed the approved scale by more than 20%; the legal documents
between the parties meet the requirements of the assessment; and the processor has
complied strictly over the past three years with no major incident. This is
published guidance, not statutory text, and is the operative practical test for
whether an extension or a fresh declaration is needed.

`[Statutory]` Order No. 11, Art. 13: a processor that disagrees with the assessment
result may apply to the CAC for re-assessment within 15 working days of receiving
it; the re-assessment result is final.

### Standard contract

`[Statutory]` Order No. 13, Art. 7: the processor must file the standard contract
with its provincial cyberspace administration **within 10 working days of the
contract taking effect**, submitting the contract and the impact assessment report,
and is responsible for the authenticity of the filing materials.

`[Statutory]` Order No. 13, Art. 5: a personal information protection impact
assessment must be carried out before the export.

### Certification

`[Statutory]` Order No. 20, Art. 6: before applying for certification, the
processor must perform the notification, separate consent and impact assessment
obligations required by laws and administrative regulations.

`[Statutory]` Order No. 20, Art. 4: the CAC, with the national data administration
and other departments, makes the certification standards and technical
specifications; SAMR, with the CAC, makes the certification rules and the unified
certificate and mark.

`[Dynamic — retrieve current version]` Certification is performed by professional
bodies that have completed filing, and the applicable certification standard has
been changed by joint announcement since Order No. 20 took effect. This reference
does not state which bodies are filed or which standard edition applies. Retrieve
the current announcements from cac.gov.cn (§7).

`[Dynamic — retrieve current version]` The declaration guide for the security
assessment and the filing guide for the standard contract are issued in numbered
editions and revised. Do not work from an edition number quoted in secondary
material; retrieve the current edition from cac.gov.cn and the declaration portal.
